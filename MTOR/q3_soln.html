A part of a circle centered at the origin with radius \(r=7\) cm is given in the figure (A) below. A right triangle is formed in the first quadrant (see Figure (A)). One of its sides lies on the \(x\)-axis. Its hypotenuse runs from the origin to a point on the circle. The hypotenuse makes an angle \(\theta \) with the \(x\)-axis. Assume that the angle \(\theta \) changes at the rate \(\frac {d\theta }{dt}=0.2\) radians per second.

(a)
Label Figure A.

(b)
In Figure B, draw the the triangle twice; once when \(\theta \) is small and once more, when \(\theta \) is close to \(\frac {\pi }{2}\).

(c)
Find the rate of change of the height of the triangle when \(\theta =\frac {\pi }{3}\)
\(y=7\sin {\theta }\)
\begin{align*} \frac {dy}{dt}&=\frac {dy}{d\theta }\cdot \frac {d\theta }{dt}\\ \frac {dy}{dt}&=7\cos {\theta } \cdot \frac {d\theta }{dt}\\ \eval {\frac {dy}{dt}}_{\theta =\frac {\pi }{3}}&=7(1/2)(0.2)\\ &=0.7 \text {cm/sec} \end{align*}
(d)
Find the rate of change of the area of the triangle when \(\theta =\frac {\pi }{3}\)
First, we need to find \(x\): \(x=7 \cos {\theta }\). From part c, we know \(y=7\sin {\theta }\)
\begin{align*} A&=\frac {1}{2}xy\\ A&=\frac {49}{2}\cos (\theta )\sin (\theta )\\ \frac {dA}{dt}&=\frac {49}{2}(-\sin ^2(\theta )+\cos ^2(\theta )) \cdot \frac {d\theta }{dt}\\ \eval {\frac {dA}{dt}}_{\theta =\frac {\pi }{3}}&=\frac {49}{2}\left (-\left (\frac {\sqrt {3}}{2}\right )^2+\left (\frac {1}{2}\right )^2 \right ) \cdot 0.2\\\\ &=\frac {49}{2}\cdot \frac {-1}{2} \cdot 0.2\\ &=\frac {-4.9}{2} cm^2/sec \end{align*}
