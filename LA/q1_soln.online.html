(a)
Find the linearization, \(L(x)\), of the function \(f(x) = e^{2x}\) at \(a = 0\).
Recall that \(L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)\).
Hence \(f'(x) = 2e^{2x} \implies f'(0) = 2e^{0} = 2\) and \( f(0) = e^{0} = 1\).
Therefore \(L(x) = 1 + 2\cdot x\)
(b)
Using the linearization, \(L(x)\), from the part (a), approximate \(e\).
\begin{align*} e &= e^{2\cdot (1/2)}\\ &= f(1/2) \approx L(1/2)\\ &\approx 1 + 2\cdot (1/2)\\ &\approx 2 \end{align*}
(c)
Is the estimation found in part (b) an overestimate or an underestimate? EXPLAIN.
\(f''(x) = 4e^{2x}\) is positive on the interval \(\left (0, \dfrac {1}{2}\right )\). That means the graph of \(f\) is concave up between \(x=0\) and \(x=\dfrac {1}{2}\), so this estimate is an underestimate. (The graph of the linearization \(L\) lies below the graph of the function \(f\).)
