Let \(f(x)=\frac {\ln (x)}{x - 2}\).
(a)
Evaluate the limit.
\[ \lim _{x \to 2^-} \frac {\ln (x)}{x - 2}\]
This limit is of the form \(\frac {\#}{0}\), the numerator positive, and the denominator is negative. Therefore, \( \lim _{x \to 2^-} \frac {\ln {x}}{x - 2} = -\infty \).
(b)
Find the vertical asymptotes of \(f\). EXPLAIN and justify your answer.
\(x=2\) is a vertical asymptote of \(f\) since \(\lim _{x \to 2^-} \frac {\ln {x}}{x - 2} = -\infty \) calculated in part (a).
On the other hand, we know that
\(\displaystyle \lim _{x \to 0^+} \ln (x)=-\infty \).
So, it follows that
\(\displaystyle \lim _{x \to 0^+} \frac {\ln (x)}{x - 2}=\infty \),
since the limit is of the form \(\frac {\infty }{\#}\), the numerator negative, and the denominator negative. That means \(x=0\) is a vertical asymptote of \(f\) since \(\lim _{x \to 0^+} \frac {\ln {x}}{x - 2} = \infty \)
