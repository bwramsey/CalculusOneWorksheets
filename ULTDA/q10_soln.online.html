For the piecewise function \(f\) defined by

\(f(x) = \left \{ \begin {array}{cl} \sin (x) & \qquad \text {if }\hspace {0.1in} x \leq 0 \\ \\ \dfrac {x^{2}}{x^{2}-4} & \qquad \text {if } \hspace {0.1in} 0< x< 2 \\ \\ \dfrac {x^{2}}{x^{2}+4} & \qquad \text {if } \hspace {0.1in} 2 \leq x \end {array} \right . \)

(a)
Find all vertical asymptotes. EXPLAIN and justify your answer by using appropriate limits.
Candidates for vertical asymptote: \(x^{2}-4 = 0 \implies x= -2\), \(x=2\) (Each piece of \(f\) is continuous at all other points).

Note: \(x = -2\) is not a vertical asymptote, since \(f(x)=\sin {(x)}\) near \(-2\), so \(\displaystyle \lim {x\to -2} f(x) = \displaystyle \lim {x\to -2} \sin (x) = \sin (-2)\).

\[\displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-} \frac {x^{2}}{x^{2}-4}=\displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-} \frac {x^{2}}{(x-2)(x+2)}= -\infty \]

Note: the limit is of the form \( \frac {\#}{0}\), the numerator positive, and the denominator negative.

The line \(x=2\) is a vertical asymptote of \(f\) because \(\displaystyle \lim {x\to 2^-} f(x) = -\infty \). This is the only vertical asymptote of \(f\).

(b)
Find all horizontal asymptotes. EXPLAIN and justify your answer by using appropriate limits.
Check End Behavior as \(x \to \infty \):

\(\displaystyle \lim {x \to \infty }\frac {x^{2}}{x^{2}+4}\) is of the form: \(\frac {\infty }{\infty }\)
\begin{align*} \displaystyle \lim {x \to \infty } \frac {x^{2}}{x^{2}+4} &= \displaystyle \lim {x \to \infty } \frac {x^{2}}{x^{2}+4} \cdot \frac {\frac {1}{x^{2}}}{\frac {1}{x^{2}}} \\ &= \displaystyle \lim {x \to \infty } \frac {1}{1+\frac {4}{x^{2}}}\\ &= 1 \\ \end{align*}

The line \(y = 1\) is a horizontal asymptote of \(f\) since \(\displaystyle \lim {x \to \infty } f(x) = 1\).

Check End Behavior as \(x \to -\infty \):

\(\displaystyle \lim {x \to -\infty }\sin (x)\) does not exist, since the function oscillates.
For example, \(f(-n\pi )=\sin {(-n\pi )}=0\), while \(f(-(2n+1)\frac {\pi }{2})=\sin {\Bigl (-(2n+1)\frac {\pi }{2}\Bigr )}=1\), for all positive integers \(n\).

There is no horizontal asymptote as \(x \to -\infty \) since \(\displaystyle \lim {x \to -\infty } f(x)\) does not exist.
