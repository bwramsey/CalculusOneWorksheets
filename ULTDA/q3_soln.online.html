Let \(f\) be a function given by \(f(x)=\ln (1+x)\).
(a)
Find the domain of \(f\). Write your answer in interval notation.
\(1+x>0 \implies x>-1 \implies \text {domain:}(-1,+\infty )\)
(b)
Find the vertical asymptotes of \(f\) and EXPLAIN and justify your answer.
The function \(f(x) = \ln (1+x)\) has a vertical asymptote at \(x=-1\) since \(\displaystyle \lim _{x \to -1^+} \ln {(1+x)}=-\infty \). (There is no Theorem/Test to reference here, since it is checking the definition of a vertical asymptote.)
Alternatively, \(g(x)=\ln (x)\) has a vertical asymptote at \(x=0\). \(f\) is \(g\) shifted one unit left so \(f\) will have a vertical asymptote at \(x=-1\).
