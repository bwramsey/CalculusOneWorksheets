Let \(f\) be a function that is continuous on its domain \((-6,4)\). The graph of \(f'\), the derivative of \(f\), is given below.

Based on the graph of \(f'\), answer the question below.

(a)
List x-coordinates of all critical points of \(f\).
Notice, \(f'(-4)=f'(1)=f'(3)=0\), and \(f'(0)\) is not defined. Therefore, the function \(f\) has critical points at \(x=-4\), \(x=0\), \(x=1\) and \(x=3\).
(b)
List x-coordinates of all critical points of \(f\) where \(f'(x)=0\).
\(x=-4\), \(x=1\) and \(x=3\).
(c)
List x-coordinates of all critical points of \(f\) where \(f'(x)\) is undefined.
\(x=0\)
(d)
List x-coordinates of all local minimums of \(f\).
\(x=-4\) and \(x=3\). At these points the sign of \(f'(x)\) changes from negative to positive.
(e)
List x-coordinates of all local maximums of \(f\).
\(x=1\) At this point the sign of \(f'(x)\) changes from positive to negative.
(f)
List all intervals where \(f\) is decreasing and concave down.
\((1,2)\) Notice, \(f'(x)\) is negative and decreasing there.
(g)
List all intervals where \(f\) is decreasing and concave up.
\((-6,-4)\) and \((2,3)\). Notice, \(f'(x)\) is negative and increasing there.
(h)
List all intervals where \(f\) is increasing and concave down.
\((0,1)\) Notice, \(f'(x)\) is positive and decreasing there.
(i)
List all intervals where \(f\) is increasing and concave up.
\((-4,0)\) and \((3,4)\). Notice, \(f'(x)\) is positive and increasing there.
(j)
List x-coordinates of all inflection points of \(f\).
\(x=0\), and \(x=2\) \(f'(x)\) is increasing on \((-6,0)\) and decreasing on \((0,2)\), then increasing on \((2,4)\).
