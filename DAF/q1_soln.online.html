(a)

Suppose \(f'(2)\) exists. Which of the following must be true?

(i)
\(\lim _{x \to 2} f(x)\) must exist, but \(\lim _{x \to 2} f(x) \ne f(2)\)
(ii)
\(\lim _{x \to 2} f(x) = f(2)\).
(iii)
\(\lim _{x \to 2} f(x) = f'(2)\)
(iv)
\(\lim _{x \to 2} f(x)\) need not exist.
The correct answer is (ii):
\begin{align*} \mbox {$f'(2)$ exists} &\iff \mbox {$f$ differentiable at $x = 2$}\\ &\implies \mbox {$f$ continuous at $x = 2$}\\ &\iff \lim _{x \to 2} f(x) = f(2) \end{align*}
(b)

Assuming that \(\lim _{x \to 0} \frac {\sin (x)}{x} = 1\), which of the following is true?

(i)
\(\frac {0}{0} = 1\)
(ii)
the tangent line to \(y = \sin (x)\) at \((0,0)\) has slope \(1\).
(iii)
you can cancel the \(x\)’s.
(iv)
for all \(x\) near \(0\), \(\sin (x) = x\).
(v)
for all \(x\) near \(0\), \(\sin (x) \approx x\).
This problem has two correct answers: (ii) and (v).

Statement (ii) is true:

\begin{align*} f'(0) &= \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {\sin (0 + h) - \sin (0)}{h} \textrm { Which has form } \ZeroOverZero \\ &= \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {\sin (h)}{h} \\ &= 1\\ &\implies \mbox {slope of tangent line at $(0,0)$ is $1$} \end{align*}

Statement (v) is true: When \(x\) is near \(0\) the tangent line \(y = x\) is a good approximation to \(f\).
