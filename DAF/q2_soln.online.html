(b)
Let
\[ f(x) = \frac {1}{x + 4}. \]
Use the (limit) definition of derivative in (a) to find \(f'(x)\).
\begin{align*} f'(x) &= \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {f(x + h) - f(x)}{h} \\ & = \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {\frac {1}{x+h +4}-\frac {1}{x+4}}{h} \textrm { which has form } \ZeroOverZero \\ &= \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {\frac {x+4 -(x+h +4)}{(x+h +4)(x+4)}}{h}\\ &= \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {1}{h} \cdot \frac {-h}{(x+h +4)(x+4)} \\ &= \lim _{h \to 0} \frac {-1}{(x+h +4)(x+4)} = \frac {-1}{(x+4)^2} \end{align*}
