True or False:
(1)
If \(f(x) = (x-2)^x\), then \(f'(x) = x (x-2)^{x-1}\).
False. Any time that you have a function of \(x\) raised to a function of \(x\), in order to compute the derivative you need to use logarithmic differentiation (or something equivalent).

Correct derivative of \(f\):

\begin{align*} f(x) &= (x-2)^x \implies f(x) = e^{x \ln (x-2)} \\ &\implies f'(x) = e^{x \ln (x-2)} \cdot \left (1\cdot \ln (x-2) + x \cdot \frac {1}{x-2}\right ) \\ &\implies f'(x) = (x-2)^x \cdot \left (\ln (x-2) + \frac {x}{x-2}\right ) \end{align*}
(2)
If \(f(x) = (3x)^x\), then \(f'(x) = (3x)^x \ln (3x)\).
False. Same as part (1).

Correct derivative of \(f\):

\begin{align*} f(x) &= (3x)^x \implies f(x) = e^{x \cdot \ln (3x)} \\ &\implies f'(x) = e^{x \cdot \ln (3x)} \left (1 \cdot \ln (3x) + x \cdot \frac {3}{3x} \right ) \\ &\implies f'(x) = (3x)^x \left (\ln (3x) + 1 \right ) \end{align*}
