A piecewise defined function \(f\) is given by

\(f(x) = \left \{ \begin {array}{cl} \frac {2 x-3}{x-2} & \qquad \text {if }\hspace {0.1in} x < 2 \\ \\ \frac {x^2-5x +6}{x^2-4} & \qquad \text {if } \hspace {0.1in} x>2 \\ \\ \end {array} \right . \)
Determine the form of the limit, then find the limit.

(a)
\( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+} f(x)\)

Since \( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+} f(x)= \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+}\frac {x^2-5x +6}{x^2-4}\), the form of the limit is \(\frac {0}{0}\).
\( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+} f(x)= \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+}\frac {x^2-5x +6}{x^2-4}= \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+}\frac {(x-3)(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+2)}= \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+}\frac {x-3}{x+2}=-\frac {1}{4}\)

(b)
\( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-} f(x)\)

Since \( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-} f(x)= \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-}\frac {2x-3}{x-2}\), the form of the limit is \(\frac {\#}{0}\).
\( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-} f(x)= \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-}\frac {2x-3}{x-2}= -\infty \),
since the numerator positive, the denominator negative and goes to 0.

(c)
\( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2} f(x)\)
The limit does not exist, because \( \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^+} f(x)\ne \displaystyle \lim {x \to 2^-} f(x)\).
