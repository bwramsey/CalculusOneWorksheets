Some notes on notation for finding limits:

(a)
The first step in evaluating any limit is to find the form. This is true, even if the question does not specifically ask for the form.
(b)
To find the form of \(\displaystyle \lim {x \to a}\dfrac {f(x)}{g(x)}\), take the limits of the numerator and denominator SEPARATELY. Do not “plug in \(x=a\)”. “Plugging in” can not occur until after the function is indicated to be continuous.
(c)
When writing a form of a limit, we NEVER write \(\displaystyle \lim {x \to a}\dfrac {f(x)}{g(x)}=\frac {0}{0}\). It is the \(=\) that makes this a mathematically incorrect statement. Instead, write \(\displaystyle \lim {x \to a}\dfrac {f(x)}{g(x)}\) is of the form \(\frac {0}{0}\).
(d)
When evaluating limits, do NOT drop the \(\displaystyle \lim {x \to a}\) until you have evaluated the limit. For example, the following is INCORRECT:

\(\displaystyle \lim {x \to 2}\frac {2x^2-4x}{x-2}=\frac {2x(x-2)}{x-2}=2x=4\)

Instead, we write:

\(\displaystyle \lim {x \to 2}\frac {2x^2-4x}{x-2}=\displaystyle \lim {x \to 2}\frac {2x(x-2)}{x-2}=\displaystyle \lim {x \to 2}2x=4\)
