Suppose that \(f(5) = 7\), \(f'(5) = 8\), \(g(5) = 3\), and \(g'(5) = -4\). Find:
(a)
\((fg)'(5)\).
\begin{align*} (fg)'(5) &= (f'(5) \cdot g(5)) + (f(5) \cdot g'(5)) \\ &= (8)(3) + (7)(-4) \\ &= 24 - 28 = -4. \end{align*}
(b)
\(\left [ \dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {f}{g} \right ) \right ]_{x=5}\)
\begin{align*} \left [ \dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {f}{g} \right ) \right ] (5) &= \frac {(g(5) \cdot \left [ \dfrac {d}{dx} f \right ] (5)) - (f(5) \cdot \left [ \dfrac {d}{dx} g \right ](5))}{(g(5))^2} \\ &= \frac {(3)(8) - (7)(-4)}{3^2} \\ &= \frac {24 + 28}{9} = \frac {52}{9}. \end{align*}
(c)
\( \left ( \frac {g}{f} \right )' (5)\)
\begin{align*} \left ( \frac {g}{f} \right )' (5) &= \frac {(f(5) \cdot g'(5)) - (g(5) \cdot f'(5))}{(f(5))^2} \\ &= \frac {(7)(-4) - (3)(8)}{7^2} \\ &= \frac {-28 - 24}{49} = - \frac {52}{49}. \end{align*}
(d)
\(\eval { \dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {g(x)}{x+2} \right )}_{x=5}\)
\begin{align*} \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {g(x)}{x+2} \right )}_{x=5} &= \eval {\frac {(x+2) \cdot g'(x)) - (g(x) \cdot (x+2)'}{(x+2)^2}}_{x=5} \\ &= \frac {(5+2)(-4) - (3)(1)}{(5+2)^2} \\ &= \frac {(7)(-4) - (3)(1)}{7^2} \\ &= \frac {-28 - 3}{49} = - \frac {31}{49} \end{align*}
(e)
\(\eval { \dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {xf(x)}{g(x)} \right )}_{x=5}\)
\begin{align*} \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {xf(x)}{g(x)} \right )}_{x=5} &= \eval {\frac {(((xf(x))' \cdot g(x)) - (g'(x) \cdot (xf(x)))}{(g(x))^2}}_{x=5} \\ &= \eval {\frac {\left ( (x)' \cdot f(x)+x \cdot f'(x) \right ) \cdot g(x)) - (g'(x) \cdot (xf(x))}{(g(x))^2}}_{x=5} \\ &= \frac {(1 \cdot 7+ 5 \cdot 8)(3) - (-4)(5)(7)}{(3)^2} \\ &= \frac {141+140}{9} \\ &= \frac {281}{9} \end{align*}
(f)
\(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 5} \dfrac {x^2 g(x) - 75 }{x-5}\). EXPLAIN.

We first note that this limit has form \(\ZeroOverZero \), but with no formula for \(g(x)\) there is no simplification we can perform. Notice that if we set \(w(x) = x^2 g(x)\), then \(w(5) = (5)^2 g(5) = 75\). This limit is the definition of the derivative, \(w'(5)\).

\begin{align*} \lim _{x\to 5} \dfrac {x^2 g(x) - 75 }{x-5} &= w'(5) \\ &= \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx}( x^2 g(x) )}_{x=5}\\ &= \eval {2x g(x) + x^2 g'(x) }_{x=5}\\ &= 2(5) g(5) + (5)^2 g'(5)\\ &= -70 \end{align*}

\(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 5} \dfrac {x^2 g(x) - 75 }{x-5} = -70\) by the product rule since this is the definition of the derivative of \(x^2 g(x)\) at \(x=5\).

(g)
\(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 5} \dfrac {\frac {x+2}{f(x)} - 1 }{x-5}\). EXPLAIN.

We first note that this limit has form \(\ZeroOverZero \). Notice that if we set \(k(x) = \frac {x+2}{f(x)}\), then \(k(5) = \frac {5+2}{f(5)} = 1\). This limit is the definition of the derivative, \(k'(5)\).

\begin{align*} \lim _{x\to 5} \dfrac {\frac {x+2}{f(x)} - 1 }{x-5} &= k'(5) \\ &= \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx} \left ( \frac {x+2}{f(x)} \right )}_{x=5}\\ &= \eval { \dfrac {f(x) - (x+2)f'(x)}{(f(x))^2} }_{x=5}\\ &= \dfrac {f(5) - (5+2)f'(5)}{(f(5))^2}\\ &= \dfrac {7 - 7(8)}{(7)^2}\\ &= -1 \end{align*}

\(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 5} \dfrac {\frac {x+2}{f(x)} - 1 }{x-5} = -1\) by the quotient rule since this is the definition of the derivative of \(\frac {x+2}{f(x)}\) at \(x=5\).
