Let \(f\) and \(g\) be two functions such that \(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }f(x)=\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }g(x)=\infty \).

We say that \(f\) grows faster than \(g\) as \(x\) goes to \(\infty \) if \(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }\dfrac {f(x)}{g(x)}=\infty \), or, equivalently, if

\(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }\dfrac {g(x)}{f(x)}=0\).

If the limit exists, namely, if \(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }\dfrac {f(x)}{g(x)}=M\), for some positive number \(M\),

then we say that the functions \(f\) and \(g\) have comparable growth rates.

In other words, we compare the growth rates of functions \(f\) and \(g\) by computing the limit of the form \(\dfrac {\infty }{\infty }\): \(\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }\dfrac {f(x)}{g(x)}\) .
