(a)
True or False: If the acceleration of an object is constant, then its velocity is constant.
This is false in general, and is true if and only if the acceleration is 0. Let \(a\) denote your non-zero constant acceleration. If \(a > 0\) then your velocity is increasing, and if \(a < 0\) then your velocity is decreasing. See the accompanying picture for when \(a>0\) (note that the units for \(v(t)\) and \(a(t)\) are not the same!)
(b)
True or False: A moving object can have negative acceleration and increasing speed.
True. If your velocity is negative (which means that you are moving in the negative direction) then a negative acceleration increases the magnitude (or absolute value) of the velocity. But the magnitude of the velocity is the speed.
