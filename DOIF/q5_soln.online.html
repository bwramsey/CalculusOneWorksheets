A table of values for \(f\) and \(f'\) is shown below. Suppose that \(f\) is a one-to-one function and \(f^{-1}\) is its inverse.

\(x\) \(f(x)\) \(f'(x)\)
1 3 4
3 4 5
4 6 3
(a)
Evaluate \(f^{-1}(f(x))\) at \(x = 3\).
\( f^{-1}(f(3)) = f^{-1}(4) = 3\)
(b)
Evaluate \(\dfrac {d}{dx} f(f(x))\) at \(x = 3\).
\begin{align*} \dfrac {d}{dx} f(f(x)) = f'(f(x)) \cdot f'(x) &\implies \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx} f(f(x))}_{x = 3} = f'(f(3)) \cdot f'(3)\\ &= f'(4) \cdot 5 = 3 \cdot 5 = 15 \end{align*}
(c)
Evaluate \(\dfrac {d}{dx}\ln ((f(x))\) at \(x = 3\). [2]
\begin{align*} \dfrac {d}{dx}\ln ((f(x)) = \frac {f'(x)}{f(x)} \\ &\implies \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx} \ln (f(x))}_{x = 3} = \frac {f'(3)}{f(3)} = \frac {5}{4} \end{align*}
(d)
Evaluate \(f^{-1}(x)\) at \(x = 3\). [2]
\( f^{-1}(3) = 1 \iff 3 = f(1)\)
(e)
Evaluate \(\dfrac {d}{dx} f^{-1}(x)\) at \(x = 3\). [2]
\begin{align*} \dfrac {d}{dx} f^{-1}(x) &= \frac {1}{f'(f^{-1}(x))} \\ &\implies \eval {\dfrac {d}{dx} f^{-1}(x)}_{x = 3} = \frac {1}{f'(f^{-1}(3))} \\ &= \frac {1}{f'(1)} = \frac {1}{4} \end{align*}
(f)
Evaluate \(\lim _{x \to 4} \frac {f(x)-f(4)}{x-4}\) [2]
\(\lim _{x \to 4} \frac {f(x)-f(4)}{x-4} = f'(4)=3\)
