The velocity of an object moving along a straight line is given by \(v(t)\) (in ft/s) and we only know the following: \( \int _0^3 v(t)\d t = -3\), and \(\int _3^4 v(t)\d t = 5\).

Compute the following displacements, if possible. If it is not possible, give examples explaining why not.

(a)
\(s(3)-s(0)\)
\[ s(3)-s(0)=\int _0^3 v(t)\d t =-3 ft \]
(b)
\(s(4)-s(0) \)
First notice that
\begin{equation*} s(4)-s(0)= \int _0^4 v(t) \d t \end{equation*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation*} s(4)-s(0)=\int _0^3v(t) \d t + \int _3^4 v(t) \d t =-3+5=2 ft \end{equation*}
(c)
Find the displacement during the interval \([0,4]\) if the velocity at the time \(t\), \(0\le t\le 4\), was \(v(t)+2\) ft/s instead?
In that case, we would have that

\(s(4)-s(0)=\int _0^4(v(t)+2) \d t =\int _0^4v(t) \d t+\int _0^42\d t\)

We use the result in part (b) for the first integral, and geometry for the second interval.

\(s(4)-s(0) =2+2(4-0)=2+8=10\) ft

(d)
Find the displacement during the interval \([0,4]\) if the velocity at the time \(t\), \(0\le t\le 4\), was \(5v(t)\) ft/s instead?
\(s(4)-s(0)=\int _0^4 5v(t) \d t =5\int _0^4v(t) \d t=5(2)=10\) ft
