Find the derivative of the following functions:
(a)
\(F(x) = \int _{\sqrt {x}}^1 \frac {t^2}{2 + 3t^4} \d t\)
First notice that
\[ F(x) = \int _{\sqrt {x}}^1 \frac {t^2}{2 + 3t^4} \d t = - \int ^{\sqrt {x}}_1 \frac {t^2}{2 + 3t^4} \d t .\]
So we can apply the First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, along with the chain rule, to compute:
\begin{align*} F^\prime (x) &= \ddx \left ( - \int ^{\sqrt {x}}_1 \frac {t^2}{2 + 3t^4} \d t \right ) \\ \text {we introduce the variable:}\ u=\sqrt x \implies F'(x)&=\frac {dF}{du}\frac {du}{dx}\\ &= - \frac {u^2}{2 + 3(u)^4} \cdot \ddx (\sqrt {x}) \\ &= - \frac {u^2}{2+3u^4} \cdot \frac {1}{2 \sqrt {x}} \\ \text {substituting}\ u=\sqrt x \implies F^\prime (x) &= - \frac {x}{2+3x^2} \cdot \frac {1}{2 \sqrt {x}} \\ &= - \frac {\sqrt {x}}{2(2+3x^2)} \end{align*}
(b)
\(G(x) = \int _x^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t\)
First notice that
\begin{align*} G(x) &= \int _x^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \\ &= \int _x^0 \sin (7t^2) \d t + \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \\ &= - \int ^x_0 \sin (7t^2) \d t + \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \end{align*}

It is worth pointing out that breaking up the integral above at \(0\) was arbitrary. Since \(\sin (7t^2)\) is continuous over \((-\infty , \infty )\), we could have chosen any real number in place of \(0\).

We can now apply the First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, along with the chain rule, to compute:

\begin{align*} G^\prime (x) &= \ddx \left ( - \int ^x_0 \sin (7t^2) \d t + \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) \\ &= \ddx \left ( - \int ^x_0 \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) + \ddx \left ( \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) \end{align*}

The left integral requires no chain rule on the FFTOC. \( \ddx \left ( - \int ^x_0 \sin (7t^2) \d t \right )=-\sin (7x^2)\\\)

The right integral requires chain rule. We’ll subsitute \(u=x^3\).

\begin{align*} \ddx \left ( \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) &= \dd {u} \left ( \int _0^{u} \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) \dd [u]{x}\\ &=\sin (7u^2) \cdot \ddx (x^3)\\ &=\sin (7u^2) \cdot 3x^2\\ \text {substituting}\ u=x^3 \implies \ddx \left ( \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) &=3x^2 \sin (7x^6) \end{align*}

Combining we have:

\[G^\prime (x) = 3x^2 \sin (7x^6) - \sin (7x^2).\]

An alternative approach using SFTOC: Call \(h(t) = \sin (7t^2)\) and let \(H\) be an antiderivative of \(h\). By SFTOC, \(\int _x^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t = \eval {H(t)}_{t=x}^{x^3}\). Then:

\begin{align*} \ddx \left ( \int _0^{x^3} \sin (7t^2) \d t \right ) &= \ddx \left ( \eval {H(t)}_{t=x}^{x^3} \right )\\ &= \ddx \left ( H(x^3) - H(x) \right ) \\ &= H'(x^3) \cdot 3x^2 - H'(x)\\ &= h(x^3)\cdot 3x^2 - h(x)\\ &= \sin (7x^6) \cdot 3x^2 - \sin (7x^2) \end{align*}
