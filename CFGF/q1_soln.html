Let \(f\) be a function given by: \(\displaystyle f(x)=\left (\tan ^{-1}{(x)}\right )^{2}\).
(a)
State the domain of \(f\). Write your answer in interval notation.
Domain of \(f={\color {blue}(-\infty ,+\infty )}\)
(b)
Find the critical points of \(f\). Show your work!
Since
\(f'(x)=2\tan ^{-1}{(x)}\cdot \dfrac {1}{x^{2}+1}=\dfrac {2\tan ^{-1}{(x)}}{x^{2}+1}\),
the only critical points of \(f\) are the points where \(f'(x)=0\).
\(f'(x)=0\hspace {0.2in}\equiv \hspace {0.2in}\tan ^{-1}{(x)}=0\hspace {0.2in}\equiv \hspace {0.2in}x=0\).
Therefore, the function \(f\) has the only critical point at \(x=0\).
(c)
Show that   \(f''(x)=\dfrac {2-4x\tan ^{-1}{(x)}}{(x^2+1)^2}\).
\(f''(x)=\Big (\dfrac {2\tan ^{-1}{(x)}}{x^{2}+1}\Big )'=\dfrac {2\frac {x^{2}+1}{x^{2}+1}-2\tan ^{-1}{(x)}(2x)}{(x^{2}+1)^2}=\dfrac {2-4x\tan ^{-1}{(x)}}{(x^{2}+1)^2}\)
(d)
Use the second derivative test to determine whether critical point(s) of \(f\) correspond to local minimums, local maximums, or whether the test is inconclusive.
Since
\(f''(0)=\dfrac {2-4(0)\tan ^{-1}{(0)}}{(0^{2}+1)^2}=2>0\),
it follows by the Second derivative test that the function \(f\) has a local minimum at \(x=0\).
