Determine the value of \(\lim _{x \to 0} \left (x^2 \cos \left (\frac {1}{x}\right ) \right )\). EXPLAIN.

\(\lim _{x \to 0} \cos \left (\frac {1}{x}\right )\) does not exist, so we cannot apply the Product law.

We know that for any \(x\neq 0\) that \( -1\leq \cos \left ( \frac {1}{x}\right ) \leq 1\). Multiplying this inequality by \(x^2\) (which is positive) we find that for all \(x\) near \(0\),

\[ -x^2 \leq x^2 \cdot \cos \left ( \frac {1}{x}\right ) \leq x^2 \]
The limits of the functions on the left and right sides of this compound inequality are:
\begin{align*} \lim _{x \to 0} (-x^2) &= 0\\ \lim _{x \to 0} x^2 &= 0\\ \end{align*}

Our explanation:

\(\lim _{x \to 0} \left (x^2 \cos \left (\frac {1}{x}\right ) \right ) =0\) by the Squeeze Theorem since \(-x^2 \leq x^2 \cdot \cos \left ( \frac {1}{x}\right ) \leq x^2\) and \(\lim _{x \to 0} (-x^2) = \lim _{x \to 0} x^2 = 0\).
