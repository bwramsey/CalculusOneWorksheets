Compute the integral: \(\displaystyle \int \sec ^2(x) \tan (x) \, \d x\).
Let \(u = \tan (x)\). Then \(du = \sec ^2(x) dx\), and so
\begin{align*} \int sec^2(x) \tan (x) \, \d x &= \int u du\\ &= \dfrac {1}{2}u^2 + C\\ &= \dfrac {1}{2}\tan ^2(x) + C. \end{align*}

NOTE: The substitution \(v = \sec (x)\) would also work to solve this problem, as would the substitution \(w = \sec ^2(x)\). It is a good exercise to work these out!
