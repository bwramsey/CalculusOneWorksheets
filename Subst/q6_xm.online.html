Find the error in the following “solution", correct the error (or errors) and evaluate the integral.

Find \(\int _{-\frac {\pi }{2}}^{\frac {\pi }{2}} \cos (x)\sqrt {1-\cos ^2(x)}\d x\).
"Solution":
Since \(\sin (x)=\sqrt {1-\cos ^2(x)}\), let \(u=\sin (x)\). Then \(du=\cos (x)dx\);
when \(x=-\frac {\pi }{2}\), \(u=\sin \Bigl (-\frac {\pi }{2}\Bigr )=-1\); when \(x=\frac {\pi }{2}\), \(u=\sin \Bigl (\frac {\pi }{2}\Bigr )=1\). Therefore
\(\int _{-\frac {\pi }{2}}^{\frac {\pi }{2}} \cos (x)\sqrt {1-\cos ^2(x)}\d x=\int _{-1}^{1}u \cdot du=0\),
due to symmetry (integrating an odd function over a symmetric interval, \([-1,1]\)).
