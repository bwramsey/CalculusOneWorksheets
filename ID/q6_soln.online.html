The curve is given by the equation \(x^{1/3}+y^{2/3}=2\). Find \(\frac {d^2y}{dx^2}\).

Take the first derivative:

\[\frac {1}{3}x^{-2/3}+\frac {2}{3}y^{-1/3}\cdot \dfrac {dy}{dx}=0 \]


Solving for \(\dfrac {dy}{dx}\):
\[\dfrac {dy}{dx}=\frac {\frac {-1}{3x^{2/3}}}{\frac {2}{3y^{1/3}}}=\frac {-y^{1/3}}{2x^{2/3}}\]


Take the second derivative:
\[ \frac {d^2y}{dx^2}= \frac {-\frac {1}{3}y^{-2/3} \cdot \dfrac {dy}{dx} \cdot 2x^{2/3}+y^{1/3}\cdot \frac {4}{3}x^{-1/3}}{4x^{4/3}}\]


We need to substitute in for \(\dfrac {dy}{dx}\) we have:
\[ \frac {d^2y}{dx^2}= \frac {-\frac {1}{3}y^{-2/3} \cdot \frac {-y^{1/3}}{2x^{2/3}} \cdot 2x^{2/3}+y^{1/3}\cdot \frac {4}{3}x^{-1/3}}{4x^{4/3}}\]
